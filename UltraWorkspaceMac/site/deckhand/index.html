Deckhand 0.2 · beta · macOS 13 or later · Apple silicon and Intel

One window for the whole bar.

Deckhand replaces the vendor's desktop client on the Mac. It is written in Swift and SwiftUI, speaks the CoreBar's own protocol, and treats the bar as the device that does the work: the Mac configures it, draws for it and answers its questions.

Requirements

What you need.

MacmacOS 13 Ventura or later, Apple silicon or Intel. On macOS 26 the window uses the system's Liquid Glass styling; on older versions it looks like a standard Mac app.
BarA Sanytron UltraBar X with the CoreBar on the same Wi-Fi or LAN as the Mac. Launcher 1.0.9 or later is recommended; older launchers lack some of the bar's own cards.
ModulesOptional. VivoCube, SenseCube, KnobKey and DotKey each get their own page when attached. The PC-Boot card is not used.
USBNot needed for Deckhand at all; the cable only powers the bar. Mind the port rule: PC-LINK is 5 V only. See getting started.
Wi-Fi ADBOnly for three optional features: installing the bar apps, the presence-aware screen, and the launcher restart. adb comes from brew install --cask android-platform-tools.
AccountsNone. No sign-up, no license key, no telemetry.

The window

A sidebar per device, a page per job.

Every device page opens with a status strip that says what the device is showing right now, read back from the device rather than assumed, and, only when something is wrong, one sentence and the button that fixes it.

Screens

CoreBar

Compose the page the bar shows from its own cards: clock with your picture, weather, world time, the SenseCube's readings, Mac CPU, memory, disk and network, buttons, one card per installed app. A preview shows each screenful as the bar scrolls it. Publish writes the page and makes it the bar's start page.

Screens

VivoCube

Up to six cards, drawn on the Mac at the panel's 240 × 240, sent as one package and switched in place: on a timer, with the DotKey, or never. The page shows which card the cube reports it is on, plus maintenance: screen on or off, fix a black screen, restart, and the cube's Wi-Fi.

Controls

KnobKey and DotKey

What each control does. The knob turns the Mac's volume and its press plays or pauses Apple Music; DotKey keys step through the cube's cards and a long press brings the standard page back. Bindings travel with the CoreBar page.

Sensors

SenseCube

Temperature, humidity and light, read every five seconds while the page is open, and one button that puts all five SenseCube cards on the bar. With Wi-Fi ADB on, its presence radar keeps the bar's screen on while you are at the desk.

Library

Widgets, Apps, Screensavers, People

The cards Deckhand can draw with their settings; everything installed on the bar with one-click updates; the bar's screensavers laid out like the macOS pane; and contacts with one-click Teams, FaceTime and Mail.

Health

Status and Logs

Status asks the bar and every module what they are doing and puts problems first, each with its one button; Copy report puts it all on the clipboard. Logs show every message between the Mac and the bar, with secrets redacted.

How it works

The bar's own protocol, nothing in between.

There is no cloud relay and no vendor account. Deckhand talks to the CoreBar the way the vendor's client does, over three channels on your LAN, documented in the repository's PROTOCOL.md.

  1. Discovery.A UDP broadcast on port 5319; the bar answers with its name, serial and launcher version. Behind a VPN or on another subnet, Connect by address does the same with a unicast.
  2. Pairing.A one-time approval on the bar's screen. The key it hands out is stored in a user-only file on the Mac and the bar's TLS certificate is pinned, so an impostor on the network cannot reuse it.
  3. Control.TLS on port 7431 for everything the Mac asks the bar: pages, module properties, app installs. A persistent link on the same port answers the bar's own questions, so its Mac cards and the knob work.
  4. Uploads.HTTP on port 8080 for the cube's card packages and the page assets. The cube fetches its package over its own Wi-Fi; the bar only forwards the command.

Security and privacy

What Deckhand sends where.

The complete list. Everything else stays on your Mac. The full review of the device itself, which is a different matter, is in SECURITY.md.

The bar, on your LANUDP 5319 discovery, TCP 7431 TLS control, TCP 8080 uploads. Always.
Open-MeteoWeather and city lookup for the weather cards. No key, no account.
CoinGeckoPrices and today's chart for the coins you pick, at most five. No key.
ipwho.isApproximate location, only when macOS Location is unavailable and no city is set.
Inbound, optionalWith the bar apps switched on, the Mac serves their pages on TCP 7432, plain HTTP, read-only, LAN only, and only while the switch is on.
Wi-Fi ADB, optionalThe local adb against the bar on TCP 5555, for app installs, the presence-aware screen and the launcher restart. The bar exposes ADB to the whole LAN while it is on; switch it off when you do not use it.
Local dataThe pairing key in a 0600 file under Application Support, the module cache next to it, contacts for the People page, and a wire log capped at 5 MB with the key, the cube's Wi-Fi password and the weather key redacted.

Status

Beta, and honest about it.
