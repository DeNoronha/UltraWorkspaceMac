Deckhand · beta · macOS 13 or later · Apple silicon and Intel

Your UltraBar X, run from the Mac.

Deckhand is a native macOS app for the Sanytron UltraBar X. It finds the CoreBar on your network, puts your own pages on its 5:1 screen, draws cards on the VivoCube, binds the KnobKey and DotKey, and runs full-width live apps on the bar itself. Open source, no account, nothing leaves your LAN.

Actual size on the bar: 1424 × 280 px, 160 dpi.

What Deckhand does

The client the bar deserved on macOS.

The vendor's desktop app is thin on the Mac. Deckhand speaks the CoreBar's own protocol (UDP discovery, TLS control channel, HTTP uploads) and keeps the device doing the work: the bar renders its own cards, the Mac only configures it and supplies what the Mac knows.

CoreBar

Pages on the big screen

Compose pages from the bar's own cards: clock with your cover picture, weather, world time, the SenseCube's five readings, Mac CPU, memory, disk and network, buttons, and one card per installed app. Publish, switch, and make Deckhand's page the start page.

VivoCube

Cards on the cube

Up to six cards drawn at the panel's real 240 × 240, chosen from weather, music, Claude Code, crypto, calendar, date and your own picture. Uploaded once as one package and switched in place, with no flashing rewrite.

KnobKey · DotKey

Bindings that make sense

Turn the knob for the Mac's volume, press it to play or pause Apple Music. DotKey 1 and 2 step through the cube's cards, holding key 3 brings back the standard page. One button on the page publishes the bindings to the bar.

SenseCube

A screen that knows you're there

With Wi-Fi ADB on, Deckhand follows the SenseCube's presence radar: the bar's screen stays on while someone is at the desk and goes dark once the desk has been empty for a while: ten minutes by default, or the time you set.

Apps

Install and update from the Mac

The Apps page lists Deckhand's own apps, the vendor marketplace and what is built into the firmware, each with the version on the bar and Install, Update and Remove buttons; one filter shows what is on the bar or has an update, and Update All takes care of the rest. Screensavers get the same treatment.

Privacy

Nothing leaves your network

Deckhand talks to the bar on your LAN and to the Mac. No account, no telemetry. Weather comes from Open-Meteo and prices from CoinGecko, both without a key. The bar apps that use only public data fetch it on the bar itself. The ones that show what is on your Mac, or need a key or sign-in, ask the Mac for it over your LAN; your keys stay in the Mac's Keychain and never reach the bar. The vendor's catalogue is read only when you open the Apps or Screensavers page. The complete list is on the Deckhand page; the code is on GitHub for anyone to read.

Bar apps · version 2.6

Movement on the whole bar.

The bar's own cards are small and static. Deckhand ships its own Android apps for the CoreBar that take the full 1424 × 280 screen and move in near real time. Twelve of them are below; flights overhead, sun and moon, headlines, a verse of the day, your Linear tasks, the whole Earth live, your website's SEO, your Kraken portfolio, the status of the cloud services you use, your GitHub Actions runs, your Jira board, your heating, your books, your Mac's network traffic and how your connection is doing are on the bar apps page. Every app carries its own page. The ones that use only public data, such as weather, crypto and Teletekst, fetch it themselves and keep running with the Mac off; the others find Deckhand on your Mac by themselves and ask it for what only the Mac knows.

Monitor

Your Mac, live: CPU with a two-minute history, network up and down, memory and disk bars, the time. Refreshed every second.

Haven

Ships at the ports and inland terminals you choose, live from AIS: how many are on their way in, how many lie alongside, the first few by name with an arrival time. Tap a place for its approach.

Tide

Dutch water levels from Rijkswaterstaat: up to four gauges with the level now in cm NAP, its trend, today's curve against the predicted tide and the next high and low water, plus sun and moon. Runs on the bar, no key.

Schiphol

Amsterdam Schiphol as the tower sees it: which runways are in use for landing and take-off, worked out live from open ADS-B data, the aircraft around them and the last 15 minutes' movements. With a free Schiphol key, the next arrivals and departures too.

Teletekst

NOS Teletekst as it looks on television, split into two columns for the wide screen. The pages turn by themselves; tap for the next or previous one. You choose the pages.

Training

Your own activities: the latest with its route, the ones before it, this week against last week per sport and the year so far. Connect once with your own Strava API application. Powered by Strava.

Whisky Portfolio

Your whisky collection at auction prices: what it is worth together, against what you paid and a month ago, with a trend per bottle. Prices from the Whiskystats Data API with your own key, kept within its monthly credits.

Claude

Which terminal needs you. Every Claude Code session on the Mac becomes a card; the ones waiting for an answer pulse orange, with the project, the question and how long ago.

Crypto

Up to five coins in your currency, each with a sparkline of the day and the change since midnight.

Weather

Now plus three days for the Mac's location: condition, temperature, humidity, wind and chance of rain.

Music

Apple Music with album art, title, artist and album. The transport buttons work from the bar's touch screen.

Calendar

Today, tomorrow and the day after from the Mac's calendars, in the colours you already know. Past events fade.

Installed from Deckhand's Apps page over Wi-Fi ADB. Each app carries its own page. Getting started

Five minutes from DMG to your first page.

  1. Power the bar from the right port.See the warning below before you plug anything in.
  2. Put the bar on your Wi-Fi.Network setup stays on the device; Deckhand talks to it over the LAN, never over USB.
  3. Open Deckhand and look for CoreBars.Discovery is a UDP broadcast on port 5319. Behind a VPN or on another subnet, use Connect by address.
  4. Approve the pairing on the bar.The bar shows a prompt for about thirty seconds. The key it hands out is stored in your Mac's Keychain.
  5. Publish your first page.Pick cards on the CoreBar page and press Publish. Add the Monitor app from the Apps page if you want the whole bar to move.

Read this before you plug it in

The two USB ports are not interchangeable.

PortUse it forRule
USB-OTGPower: a PD or QC fast charger, or the 12 V adapter.Fast charging is fine here.
PC-LINKYour computer, and 5 V power from it.5 V only.

A phone or laptop fast charger on PC-LINK puts 9 to 20 V on a 5 V port and will permanently damage the mainboard. A computer's USB port on PC-LINK is safe and correct. Deckhand repeats this in the app.

FAQ

Questions people ask.

How do I close a bar app?

Press the bar's Back button: the small round button in the bezel, just to the right of the screen. It is easy to miss, but it is the bar's own way back, and the vendor's apps use it too, so Deckhand's apps have no close button of their own. Holding a finger on the page for a second also takes you back.

The VivoCube stops taking new cards.

Usually the bar's launcher, not the cube: after many uploads, or after a long time running, it stops passing cards on, saying it is out of upload slots or that the cube is offline. With Wi-Fi ADB on, Deckhand checks that the cube still answers and restarts the launcher by itself; the bar's screen reloads for a few seconds and the cards are sent again. Without ADB, use Restart Bar Launcher… in Bar System, re-seat the cube, or restart the bar.

Installing a bar app fails after the bar restarted.

Android turns Wi-Fi ADB off at every restart. In Deckhand, Settings → Debug Bridge → Keep On After Reboots keeps it on. See the manual for the steps on the bar.

A bar app says "Waiting for the Mac…".

Monitor, Claude, Music, Calendar, Tasks, Haven, Training, Whisky Portfolio, Schiphol, SEO, Portfolio, GitHub Actions, Jira Board, Climate, Books, Traffic and Connection show things only your Mac has, so Deckhand has to be running and on the same network as the bar. The other apps work without the Mac.
