Deckhand 0.1 · beta · macOS 13 or later · Apple silicon and Intel

Your UltraBar X, run from the Mac.

Deckhand is a native macOS app for the Sanytron UltraBar X. It finds the CoreBar on your network, puts your own pages on its 5:1 screen, draws cards on the VivoCube, binds the KnobKey and DotKey, and runs full-width live apps on the bar itself. Open source, no account, nothing leaves your LAN.

Actual size on the bar: 1424 × 280 px, 160 dpi.

What Deckhand does

The client the bar deserved on macOS.

The vendor's desktop app is thin on the Mac. Deckhand speaks the CoreBar's own protocol (UDP discovery, TLS control channel, HTTP uploads) and keeps the device doing the work: the bar renders its own cards, the Mac only configures it and supplies what the Mac knows.

CoreBar

Pages on the big screen

Compose pages from the bar's own cards: clock with your cover picture, weather, world time, the SenseCube's five readings, Mac CPU, memory, disk and network, buttons, and one card per installed app. Publish, switch, and make Deckhand's page the start page.

VivoCube

Cards on the cube

Seven cards drawn at the panel's real 240 × 240: weather, music, Claude Code, crypto, calendar, date, picture. Uploaded once as one package and switched in place every interval, with no flashing rewrite.

KnobKey · DotKey

Bindings that make sense

Press the knob to play or pause Apple Music. DotKey 1 and 2 step through the cube's cards, holding key 3 brings back the standard page. One button on the page publishes the bindings to the bar.

SenseCube

A screen that knows you're there

With Wi-Fi ADB on, Deckhand follows the SenseCube's presence radar: the bar's screen stays on while someone is at the desk and goes dark after a quiet ten minutes, which you set yourself.

Apps

Install and update from the Mac

The Apps page lists what is on the bar in four groups: installed apps, Deckhand's own apps with version numbers and one-click updates, the vendor marketplace, and what is built into the firmware.

Privacy

Nothing leaves your network

Deckhand talks to the bar on your LAN and to the Mac. No account, no telemetry, no cloud. Weather comes from Open-Meteo and prices from CoinGecko, both without a key. The code is on GitHub for anyone to read.

Bar apps · version 1.2

Movement on the whole bar.

The bar's own cards are small and static. Deckhand ships six Android apps for the CoreBar that take the full 1424 × 280 screen and move in near real time. Each one is a thin kiosk window that finds Deckhand on your Mac by itself, so the Mac does the thinking and the bar only draws.

Monitor

1.2

Your Mac, live: CPU with a two-minute history, network up and down, memory and disk bars, the time. Refreshed every second.

Claude

1.2

Which terminal needs you. Every Claude Code session on the Mac becomes a card; the ones waiting for an answer pulse orange, with the project, the question and how long ago.

Crypto

1.2

Up to five coins in your currency, each with a sparkline of the day and the change since midnight.

Weather

1.2

Now plus three days for the Mac's location: condition, temperature, humidity, wind and chance of rain.

Music

1.2

Apple Music with album art, title, artist and album. The transport buttons work from the bar's touch screen.

Calendar

1.2

Today, tomorrow and the day after from the Mac's calendars, in the colours you already know. Past events fade.

Installed from Deckhand's Apps page over Wi-Fi ADB. Each app opens a page the Mac serves on your LAN (port 7432); the bar never talks to the internet for them.

Getting started

Five minutes from DMG to your first page.

  1. Power the bar from the right port.See the warning below before you plug anything in.
  2. Put the bar on your Wi-Fi.Network setup stays on the device; Deckhand talks to it over the LAN, never over USB.
  3. Open Deckhand and look for CoreBars.Discovery is a UDP broadcast on port 5319. Behind a VPN or on another subnet, use Connect by address.
  4. Approve the pairing on the bar.The bar shows a prompt for about thirty seconds. The token is stored in a protected file on your Mac.
  5. Publish your first page.Pick cards on the CoreBar page and press Publish. Add the Monitor app from the Apps page if you want the whole bar to move.

Read this before you plug it in

The two USB ports are not interchangeable.

PortUse it forRule
USB-OTGPower: a PD or QC fast charger, or the 12 V adapter.Fast charging is fine here.
PC-LINKYour computer, and 5 V power from it.5 V only.

A phone or laptop fast charger on PC-LINK puts 9 to 20 V on a 5 V port and will permanently damage the mainboard. A computer's USB port on PC-LINK is safe and correct. Deckhand repeats this in the app.
